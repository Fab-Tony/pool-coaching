What planning hands to execution

  1. Aim. Where I'm sending the cue ball, allowing for deflection, spin and throw.
  2. Contact point. Where I hit the cue ball: the tip position.
  3. Stroke. The type of stroke, and how much power.
What aim am I applying? Where am I hitting the cue ball? And what stroke do I want to do, with how much power? Once I determine that, we move into execution mode to implement that plan.Tony

01 · Routine

The break

Its own routine: set up, rehearse, execute.

6″

A perfectly executed break

The cue ball pops about six inches, the pack spreads, the cue ball finishes centre table, and a ball goes down.

Set up

  1. Rack tight. Every ball touching. Always use a magic rack.
  2. Spot the cue ball just behind the baulk line, centre, then one finger to the left.
  3. Break cue, chalked.

Rehearse

  1. Get down and align on the centre of the head ball.
  2. Stand up and rehearse. Practise the wrist flick in the air a couple of times. Feel the cue and visualise the stroke you're committing to.

Execute

  1. Raise the bridge so the cue angles down into the cue ball, one tip below centre.
  2. Bridge long enough to strike through the ball.
  3. Grip far enough back that the pendulum is at the bottom of its swing at contact.
  4. Tip close to the cue ball at address, even with the longer bridge.
  5. Strike: swing the arm down, loose wrist, power from a flick of the wrist. No tension. Hit down into the ball.
Swing with a loose wrist, so you're almost throwing the cue but not throwing the cue. A loose grip, and the flick of the wrist is where you get the power.Tony

Progress, 17 Nov: a good break about half the time. Joe's break notes are on the Coaching page.

02 · Planning

The table scan

The questions I ask, in order, every time I come to the table.

  1. What's actually on? What balls can I pot, and what are my options? This comes before anything else.
  2. After the break: which group? The more run-out-able one, with no problems or with problems that are easy to solve.
  3. Can I run out? If yes, plan every shot to the black, now.
  4. If not, why not? If I can't run out, there's a problem on the table. Find the problem balls.
  5. What's my plan for this visit? Run out · pot, pot, safety · solve a problem · safety only.
  6. Can this visit improve the table for later? Or is it damage control with a safety?
You come to the table and ask, can I run out? If not, pick a short plan that either solves a problem or sets one up while keeping you safe.

Problem balls

Which problem first?

Ease of access, which ones have something that can cannon onto them, and which ones can be opened up.

Solve problems early

Especially with insurance balls hanging over pockets near the problem, so I can break it open and still have a shot.

On the way, or first Joe

Can I get onto the problem ball easily by solving something else? If not, solve it first.

Too many clusters to run out?

Where's my potential safety, how do I get to it, and what balls can I clear before I get there?

03 · Planning

Choosing the shot

Percentages, natural angles, the gentle follow shot, and the shots I execute best.

Not necessarily

The easiest ball on the table, or the most obvious route to the pocket.

Usually

The ball that lands me on the next ball in the run, or the one that solves a problem.

At least three balls ahead

Ideally the whole rack in 8-ball. In 9-ball or 10-ball, three ahead is about right.

Solve problems early in the run

While there's still an insurance ball to run onto.

Play to my strengths

The natural angle and the gentle follow. See the 30° rule and cue ball action on the Coaching page.

Finish on the right side Joe

Have a ball that brings me back to the side of the table I need next.

04 · Before the handoff

Risk checks

Plan so the cue ball does what I predict. If it doesn't, be in the least trouble possible.

  • Margin for error. If this goes wrong, where am I?
  • Into the line, not across it. Come into the line of the next shot.
  • Scratch. Will I go in-off? On follow shots, trace the path to the corner pocket.
  • The black. Is the shot near it? Could I pot or disturb it? Do I need to avoid it?
  • The other ball. If my route moves another ball, does it still have a pocket?

It's a cycle: if a check fails, go back to planning and choose again.

05 · Planning

Safety

A safety should improve my chances of winning the frame, not just delay my opponent.

Attacking
Go for ball in handSnooker them. I expect a foul.
Containing
Make it annoyingDistance and a hard pot. I expect a legal hit.
Defensive
Limit the damageI'm in trouble. Don't give away the frame.

“Containing safety” is a standard term. “Attacking safety” is mine. Others call it an aggressive safety or a hook.

What makes a ball safe

Block their options

A snooker, best of all a close-up one: the cue ball tight to a ball that blocks their shots.

Distance, rails and traffic

Distance between the balls, the cue ball on a rail (best with distance too), and balls in the way.

Hiding places

Behind another ball. The top cushion where the rack was. Bottom and top cushions generally, especially behind a ball. Or roll a ball to block a pocket.

Solve a problem on the way

An awkward ball on the cushion? Hit it as part of the safety and leave it better placed.

Safety planning checks

  • Trouble spot for the cue ball. Where makes it awkward, and how can I get it there?
  • Use a problem ball? Can I get there by playing one of my problem balls?
  • Their next two shots. Make both hard, not just the next one.
  • Their escapes. How many easy ways out do they have? Judge it from their side.
  • Protect my problems. Don't open up their balls while mine stay awkward.
  • Their return safety. If they reply well, am I better or worse off than now?
  • If it works, can I run out? Or do I still have problem balls?
  • Two-way shot? Attempt a pot or fix a ball, and still leave the cue ball awkward if it doesn't work out.
  • My blind spot: what if I miss it? For an attacking safety, weigh “can I pull it off?” and “what's the table if I get it wrong?”
I very rarely think about what if it goes wrong. That's a blind spot to put into my routine.Tony
