01

Mindset and routine

Most misses come from doubt, not technique.

In practice

Tune the stroke. Work on the elbow, the stance and the bridge. Try the theoretically better way.

In a match

Run the program. Do what feels comfortable on the day - forcing changes means playing badly and getting frustrated.

Comfort shifts as you practise

What feels natural now is not what felt natural two weeks or three months ago. Let it evolve - it is meant to.

Pre-shot routine on every shot

Mental and physical preparation, every time. It is the first thing to slip, so guard it.

It is certainty, not mechanics

When confident, the stroke happens naturally. After a bad run, doubt makes me misread angles I would normally see.

Trust the line

Adjusting the aim once down on the shot is a trust problem. If it feels wrong, stand up and get down again.

Check the breath

Slowing the breath before the shot changed everything.

When you're practising, you're tuning. When you're playing, you just run the program.From the 30 Oct session
02

The break

On a 7-foot table, accuracy beats power.

Accuracy over power

A 9-foot table needs power; a 7-foot table does not. An accurate break is usually already very good.

Hit the cue ball square

My breaks were drifting off to the right. The later ones were much more square.

Not too low

If the cue ball draws back off the rack, the strike was below centre.

Find your own power source

Some players use the wrist - it works if it is yours. An open bridge on the break is fine. Line up carefully, then focus on the action, not changes.

Straight-stroke check

After the follow-through, notice which part of the hand touches the table. Landing on the same spot each time means the stroke stayed straight.

03

Choosing the cue ball action

Pick the most repeatable shot that gets position.

1st choice
FollowMost repeatable
2nd
StunEasier than draw
3rd
DrawHardest to control

Avoid in-between shots

A half stop, half follow is hard to repeat on the same line. Commit to one action.

Choose for your own strengths

Good at draw: a smooth one-rail draw. Not: follow with a little side and play the short side. It looks odd but it is a sound shot.

Side as insurance

A touch of side - for example draw with a bit of right - still gets position if the main action comes up short.

The soft stop

Tip a bit lower and hit softer, so the cue ball still slides. It is pace plus feel, and only repetition teaches it.

Topspin adds speed

A rolling cue ball keeps accelerating after contact, so follow needs less cue speed than you think.

If I can get on the shot with follow, I follow. Every time.From the 30 Oct session
04

Reading angles

Thickness first, then the line - you cannot judge pace until you know the cut.

Full ballCut 0° · follow 0°
Three-quarterCut 15° · follow ~28°
Half ballCut 30° · follow ~34°
Quarter ballCut 49° · follow ~27°

Gold is the object ball; the dashed ring is where the cue ball overlaps it when you aim. “Cut” is where the object ball goes; “follow” is where a rolling cue ball goes - see the 30° rule.

Use the tangent line

A stun shot sends the cue ball off at 90° to the object ball's path. A rolling ball follows the natural angle forward from there.

Near-straight shots need more pace

A 5° cut is almost straight, so the cue ball barely moves. Cheat the pocket slightly or hit firmer than feels natural.

Close to the pocket, pot precisely

Entering on the left or right side of the pocket can change the cue ball's path by around 10°. Pick the side, then hit it.

Know, do not guess

Learn what the rails and side actually do. Players who guess are unreliable; players who know what the cue ball will do clear frames consistently.

When you know exactly what's going to happen, that's when you start winning frames consistently.From the 30 Oct session
Explainer

The natural angle - the 30° rule

Why follow is so predictable, and how to know where the cue ball goes before you hit it.

Joe calls it the natural angle or the natural line - “follow where you predict the cue ball is going.” Its textbook name is the 30° rule. Its partner for stun shots is the 90° rule, or the tangent line.

30°

The rule

When the cue ball is rolling at contact and you hit anywhere between a quarter ball and a three-quarter ball, the cue ball leaves the object ball at roughly 30° from the line you aimed along - somewhere between about 27° and 34°.

One rule covers most shots

Most cuts in a match fall between a quarter and three-quarter ball. Across that whole range the cue ball's angle barely changes, so a small error in thickness still sends the cue ball almost exactly where you planned. That is the “wide margin of error”, and it is the real reason follow is the first choice.

There are two different angles here, and they are easy to mix up. The cut angle is where the object ball goes. The 30° rule is about where the cue ball goes.

Two lines to picture on every shot

Stun - the 90° rule

A cue ball that is sliding at contact leaves along the tangent line, square to the object ball's path. Joe: “Stun, 90 degree off the tangent line.”

Follow - the 30° rule

A rolling cue ball starts along the tangent line, then the topspin bends it forward onto the natural angle, about 30° from the aim line.

In between

Anything between a stun and full roll lands somewhere between the two lines. That is the in-between shot Joe says to avoid, because it is hard to repeat.

How to use it at the table

  1. Read the thickness. Quarter, half, three-quarter? If it is between a quarter and three-quarter, the rule applies.
  2. Make it roll. Hit above centre so the cue ball is rolling, not sliding, when it arrives.
  3. Use the V. Make a peace sign. Point one finger down the aim line through the ghost ball; the other finger shows the cue ball's path, about 30° off it.
  4. Follow the line to the rail. Check where it lands for the next ball, and what it could clip or scratch into on the way. It takes the same angle off the rail.
  5. Commit. You know where it goes, so play it - do not guess.

Where the rule bends

It must be rolling

On a short shot hit at centre ball, the cue ball is still sliding at contact and behaves like a stun. Go a little higher.

Outside the band it drops fast

Thinner than a quarter or fuller than three-quarter, the angle shrinks quickly. A near-full hit follows almost straight through, which is why the 5° shot needed more pace.

Speed changes the shape, not the angle

A firm shot runs further along the tangent line before it bends; a soft one bends almost at once. The final angle is the same, but on a crowded table the pace decides whether you clip a ball on the way.

Side shows up at the rail

Side barely changes the path to the first rail. It changes what happens after - which is why a touch of side works as insurance.

Follow the natural line. Follow where you predict the cue ball is going.From the 30 Oct session
05

Planning the table

Shot by shot, analyse the situation.

Explore options in order

Follow, then draw, then stop - and the in-between shot only if nothing else works.

Play for the natural angle

Leave a natural angle on the next ball so I can follow through and remove problem balls on the way.

When unsure, err long

Too much leaves a longer shot. Too little gets me stuck behind a ball, which is far worse.

Deal with problem balls early

Clear them while the table is open. Take the trickiest ball when it is convenient, and do not play into clusters that push balls into bad spots.

The black still needs cue ball thought

A scratch on the black loses the frame. Rolling the cue ball guarantees no scratch; a stun can scratch.

English bridge at an angle

Far more stable than a makeshift bridge on awkward shots.

06

Practice drills

The shots that only repetition fixes.

Pace and feel

Soft stop

Same pot, vary the pace. Tip a little lower, hit softer, and keep the cue ball sliding.

Control

One-rail draw

Smooth draw back off one rail at controlled pace, landing in the same zone each time.

Side

Follow with side

Near-maximum side off the first rail - learn how much side sends the cue ball where.

Break

Square break

Centre-ball, square hit, no extra power. Check the cue ball does not draw back.
